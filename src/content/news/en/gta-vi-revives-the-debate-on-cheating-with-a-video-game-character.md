The relationship between Jason and Lucia in Grand Theft Auto VI has revived
an old debate. The question is whether a romance with a game character
counts as cheating. About 6 percent of British adults think it does, the BBC
writes.

## The poll

Polling company YouGov put the question to more than 4,000 British adults in
September, according to the BBC. About 6 percent would see it as cheating.
Another 19 percent would not call it cheating, but would not be okay with
it.

Women were considerably more likely than men to object, the BBC writes.
According to IGN, 4 percent of the men and 7 percent of the women see it as
cheating. Of the men, 13 percent would not be okay with it, against 24
percent of the women.

## What the game lets players do

Jason and Lucia can go shopping, visit bars and restaurants and work out
together, the BBC writes. Holding hands and answering each other's texts
quickly also builds their bond. Ignoring a message can hurt the relationship
a little, Rob Nelson of Rockstar North said in the
[full interview with IGN](/news/ign-publishes-the-full-gta-vi-interview-with-rob-nelson/).

The relationship is optional, according to IGN. Jason and Lucia want to give
it a chance at the start of the game, but players have to pursue it. If they
do not, the two still have each other's backs when it matters most.

Playing the two together feels like looking after them as a unit, Nelson
told IGN. The
[launch trailer](/news/gta-vi-gets-its-launch-trailer-five-weeks-before-release/)
shows three intimate scenes in the shower, IGN writes.

## Reactions

The BBC put its headline as a question, and that led to a backlash from
players, IGN writes. One user on X replied that video game characters are
not real. Many reactions pointed to The Sims, where players have married and
raised children for years.

The BBC spoke to Alli Franco, who posts anime and gaming content on TikTok
for more than a million followers. Franco compares it to a crush on a TV
star, but even further removed from cheating. In the end, nobody can cheat
with lines of code, Franco told the BBC.

Games are getting better at mimicking social relationships, says Arienne
Ferchaud of Florida State University to the BBC. Ferchaud studies how people
form bonds with fictional characters.

## Not new in games

Players could already marry villagers in Harvest Moon in 1996, the BBC
points out. Final Fantasy VII, Baldur's Gate II and The Sims also had
romance. Those relationships followed a fixed script. GTA VI appears to offer
something more dynamic, according to the BBC.

## What is not known

How the relationship affects the story is not known. Nelson did not want to
say more. Whether the poll of YouGov named GTA VI, the BBC and IGN do not
say. Grand Theft Auto VI comes out on 19 November for PlayStation 5 and Xbox
Series X and S.
